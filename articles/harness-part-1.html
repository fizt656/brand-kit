The Harness Is the Strategy

Part I: The model is an ingredient. Stop confusing it with the meal.

Field note / Gus Halwani

People keep asking, “What is the best model?”

Reasonable question. Increasingly, not the most useful one.

Most frontier models are already capable enough to do a surprising amount of real work. The harder problem is everything around them: the tools they can use, the context they receive, the permissions they inherit, the human decisions they interrupt, and whether anybody bothered to define what “better” means.

That whole arrangement is the harness.

Swap the model

Here is the question I find more revealing:

If you swapped the model tomorrow, what part of your system would still create value?

If the answer is “not much,” then you do not have an AI strategy. You have a vendor preference.

A good harness carries the durable parts: retrieval, tools, workflow, permissions, measurement, human judgment, feedback, and a way to recover when the model does something weird. Which it will. They all do. So do people.

Put the human somewhere specific

“Human in the loop” sounds responsible. It is also so vague that it can mean almost anything.

Which human? In which loop? Reviewing what? At what risk threshold? With the authority to change what happens next?

Those are design questions, not compliance garnish.

For one task, the human should initiate and approve. For another, review only exceptions. Somewhere else, the human may be the bottleneck we are pretending is a safeguard. You have to look at the work.

Control and emergence

The two common failures are almost opposites.

  • Control everything, route every output through three committees, and kill the value before it appears.
  • Wire up every tool, call it innovation, then discover governance after the first crater.

A useful harness has to reduce downside without flattening upside.

Musicians understand this. You need a key, a tempo, and enough shared structure to stay together. You also need room for something to happen that nobody wrote down.

AI systems need the same tension: constraints strong enough to create coherence, freedom large enough to create value, and feedback fast enough to tell the difference.

Strategy starts one layer up

Model quality matters. Of course it does.

But the model is not where your organization’s judgment lives. It is not where accountability lives. It is not where the workflow learns.

The strategic unit is the system that turns capability into a repeatable human outcome.

That is the harness. And that is where the real work starts.
